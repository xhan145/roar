Three superpowers

Dictate. Automate. Route.

Dictation is the front door. Flow is what's behind it.

01 / DICTATE

Speech in, finished writing out

Not a raw transcript — ROAR strips the "um"s, false starts and filler as you go, fixes the words it used to mishear because you taught it, and matches its tone to the app you're in. GPU-accelerated when you have one; ~half-second latency on a modern machine.

you · "so um basically the deploy is — scratch that — the release is ready"
roar → The release is ready. ✓ typed into Slack, 0.5 s
02 / AUTOMATE

Say it, and it happens

Flow rules turn phrases into actions: open an app, press a hotkey chord, insert a snippet, speak a reply, call a webhook, cue show-control gear over OSC. Rules only fire at the start of a dictation — never mid-sentence — and anything that runs code needs your explicit trust, per rule.

you · "open terminal"
roar → wt.exe launched ✓ rule: open terminal
you · "lights up"
roar → OSC /cue/go → 127.0.0.1:53000 ✓ trusted
03 / ROUTE

One dictation, everywhere it belongs

Typing into the focused app is just the default. Say "roar route notes on" and every dictation also lands in a timestamped notes file. Add the clipboard. Have it read back aloud. Meeting capture even transcribes what your PC is playing — calls and lectures — into a live, searchable local transcript.

you · "roar route notes on"
roar → routing: app + notes file ✓ session only
notes · [2026-08-01 09:14] Ship the fix after standup.

Why local matters

The privacy promise

This isn't a marketing line — it's how the app is built.

  • Your voice never leaves your machine. Transcription runs locally. There is no cloud path to disable — there isn't one.
  • No telemetry, ever — and it never phones home. The only network traffic is what you explicitly trigger: the one-time model download, an update check you click, or a webhook/OSC rule you created yourself.
  • Your licence is checked offline. No account, no sign-in, no licence server phoning home.
  • Privacy is never paid. History and audio deletion, retention controls and offline use are free in every edition — permanently.

Answers

Questions

Is Core a trial?

No. Core is free dictation you can use forever — it doesn't expire, nag, or lock up. The paid editions add workflow power on top; they don't unlock the basics.

Do I need an account or an internet connection?

No account, ever. The network is needed exactly once — to download the speech model on first run. After that, ROAR transcribes and verifies your licence entirely on your own device; dictation itself never touches the internet.

Is this a subscription?

No. It's a one-time purchase. All v1.x updates are included. A future major version may be an optional paid upgrade, and the version you bought will keep working.

What happens to my dictation history?

It stays on your machine in a local database — or nowhere at all, if you turn it off. You can delete it, and any retained audio, at any time. Those controls are free in every edition.

What's the difference between Developer and Supporter?

Functionally, nothing. Supporter has exactly the same capabilities as Developer. It exists for people who want to fund independent development — there are no exclusive Supporter features, and we won't invent any.

How do I activate a licence?

Your licence arrives as a small file. Open ROAR's Settings, go to License, choose the file, and you're done — it's verified on your own device, with no account and no internet connection. Licences are currently issued by hand after each purchase, so allow a little time; ROAR keeps working in the meantime.

Which GPUs are supported?

NVIDIA via CUDA out of the box. AMD and Intel GPUs are supported through an optional Vulkan engine you can enable in Settings. No GPU? A tuned CPU path is the default.
